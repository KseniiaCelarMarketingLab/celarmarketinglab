More Content Won’t Fix a Weak Marketing Strategy

Publishing more content cannot compensate for unclear positioning, weak audience understanding or poor channel strategy. Here is what should come first.

A company notices that its marketing is not generating enough demand.

The response is familiar:

More LinkedIn posts.

More blogs.

More videos.

More newsletters.

More Reels.

More AI-generated content.

The content calendar gets bigger.

The strategy does not.

That is the problem.

01

Content is an output, not the strategy

Content exists to communicate something.

That sounds obvious, but many marketing systems effectively work backwards.

A company starts with:

“What should we post this week?”

Instead, the strategic sequence should be closer to:

1Business objective→2audience→3positioning→4message→5channel→6content→7measurement

If the first five are unclear, producing more content simply creates more output around an unresolved problem.

The Content Marketing Institute's 2025 research illustrates this. Among B2B marketers whose content strategy was rated moderately effective or worse, common reasons included unclear goals, weak connection to the customer journey, insufficient data, ineffective audience research and an emphasis on quantity over quality.

02

Content can expose a strategic problem

Imagine a consultancy publishing three educational posts every week.

The content is well written.

Engagement is reasonable.

But the posts could belong to almost any consultancy in the category.

There is no clear point of view.

No distinctive positioning.

No obvious reason to choose this company.

The problem is not content production.

It is differentiation.

Producing 100 more posts will not automatically solve it.

03

Start with the customer problem

Good content should have a reason to exist.

If the business wants to attract founders who struggle with unclear positioning, content might explore:

  • why category language becomes generic
  • how customers perceive competing brands
  • how positioning affects product decisions
  • how to identify meaningful differentiation

Those topics are connected.

They build an intellectual territory around the business.

Compare that with publishing unrelated posts about “five marketing trends”, “three social media tips” and “why consistency matters”.

The second calendar may contain more content.

The first creates a stronger association.

04

One strategy can produce many formats

A clear strategic idea does not require publishing the same article repeatedly.

One insight can become:

  • a research article,
  • a LinkedIn analysis,
  • a short video,
  • a customer example,
  • a newsletter,
  • a sales presentation,
  • or a workshop.

This is where content systems become useful.

The strategy produces the idea.

The content system distributes it.

AI can make the second part faster.

It cannot decide whether the underlying idea is worth communicating.

05

Quantity has become easier

Generative AI has dramatically reduced the cost and time required to produce basic marketing content.

LinkedIn's 2024 B2B benchmark found that two-thirds of B2B companies were already using generative AI, while marketers reported productivity and content-creation benefits.

That creates an unusual situation.

The constraint is increasingly not:

“Can we produce enough?”

It is:

“Is what we produce worth noticing?”

The answer depends on strategy.

06

A useful content audit

Before increasing publishing frequency, ask:

  • What business objective is this content supporting?
  • Which audience problem does it address?
  • What does it communicate about our positioning?
  • Why should our audience hear this from us?
  • What action or perception should change afterwards?
  • How will we know whether it worked?

If the answers are vague, the solution is probably not another content calendar.

It is strategic clarification.

07

The best content strategy can sometimes produce less

A smaller number of highly relevant ideas can create more strategic value than a constant stream of interchangeable posts.

This is not an argument against frequency.

Frequency can matter.

The point is that frequency should amplify a strategy rather than substitute for one.

Content works best when it has somewhere to go.

Without positioning, audience understanding and a clear business objective, content becomes activity.

With them, content becomes a marketing asset.
